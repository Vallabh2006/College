India, officially the Republic of India,[j][20] is a country in South Asia.
It is the world's seventh-largest country by area and the largest by population.
[21] Bounded by the Indian Ocean on the south, the Arabian Sea on the southwest,
and the Bay of Bengal on the southeast, it shares land borders with Pakistan to
the west;[k] China, Nepal, and Bhutan to the north; and Bangladesh and Myanmar
to the east. In the Indian Ocean, India is near Sri Lanka and the Maldives.[l]

Modern humans arrived on the Indian subcontinent from Africa by 55,000 years
ago.[23] Settled life emerged in the western margins of the Indus river basin
9,000 years ago, evolving into the Indus Valley Civilisation of the third millennium
BCE.[24] By 1200 BCE, an archaic form of Sanskrit, an Indo-European language,
diffused into India from the northwest,[25] its earliest hymns containing elements
of what would become Hinduism.[26] The pre-existing Dravidian and other languages
were gradually superseded in India's northern plains.[27] Across the central stretch
of these plains, rapid urbanisation took place, and after 500 BCE a caste-based,
hereditary, form of social stratification began to solidify as Hinduism took fuller
shape.[28] During this period, Buddhism and Jainism arose, proclaiming social orders
unlinked to heredity.[29] The subsequent emergence of the loose-knit[30][31] Maurya
and Gupta Empires gave rise to wide-ranging cultural and scientific creativity.[32]
However, these eras saw a steady decline in women's status,[33] alongside the eventual
institutionalisation of untouchability.[34] In South India, across the first millennium
CE, the Middle kingdoms transmitted Dravidian language scripts and cultural traditions
to Southeast Asia.[35] Well before the millennium's end, Judaism, Christianity, Islam
and Zoroastrianism had become established on India's southern and western coasts.[36]

Early in the 2nd millennium, Muslim armies from Central Asia intermittently overran
India's northern plains,[37] eventually establishing the Delhi Sultanate, and drawing
India into the cosmopolitan networks of medieval Islam.[38] In South India, the
Vijayanagara Empire created a long-lasting composite culture.[39] During this era,
Sikhism emerged in the Punjab, rejecting established religious institutions.[40][41]
The Mughal Empire ushered in two centuries of economic expansion and relative peace,[42]
leaving a rich architectural legacy.[43] Through its gradually expanding rule, the
British East India Company consolidated territorial control over the subcontinent[44]
while transforming India into a colonial economy.[45] British Crown rule began in 1858.
Although equality before the law promised to Indians was slow to materialise,[46][47]
technological changes were introduced[48] while modern education and more organised
public life took root.[49][50] A nationalist movement emerged in India, the first in
the non-European British Empire.[51][52] Noted for nonviolent resistance after 1920,[53]
it was a major factor in ending British rule.[54] In 1947, the British Indian Empire was
partitioned into the independent dominions of a Hindu-majority India and a Muslim-majority
Pakistan. The partition brought large-scale loss of life and unprecedented migration.[55]
